In Chapter 8 of *The Adventures of Huckleberry Finn* by Mark Twain, Huck Finn escapes from his abusive father, who has been keeping him locked in a cabin. Huck cleverly plans his escape by killing a pig and spreading its blood around the cabin to make it appear that someone has murdered him. He also destroys some of the cabin's belongings to make the scene look convincing.

After preparing everything, Huck escapes in a canoe and takes some supplies with him. He travels to Jackson’s Island, where he hides from people who might be searching for him. There, he enjoys his freedom and feels relieved to be away from his father's control.

While staying on the island, Huck discovers that he is not entirely alone. He eventually finds evidence that someone else is there, which makes him curious and cautious.

This chapter highlights Huck’s cleverness, courage, and strong desire for freedom. It also shows how he uses his intelligence to survive difficult situations.

In Chapter 9 of *The Adventures of Huckleberry Finn* by Mark Twain, Huck explores Jackson’s Island and discovers a cave where he and Jim can hide. Jim reveals that he has escaped from Miss Watson because he heard that she planned to sell him. Huck is surprised to find Jim on the island, but he decides to help him.

Huck and Jim work together to make their hiding place safe. They bring their belongings into the cave to protect themselves from the weather and other dangers. Soon, a heavy storm arrives, bringing strong winds and flooding the island. The rising water carries various objects past them, including a house floating down the river. Huck and Jim explore the floating house and find useful items inside.

The chapter shows how Huck and Jim begin to trust each other and work together. It also highlights their resourcefulness and determination to survive despite the dangers around them.


In Chapter 10 of *The Adventures of Huckleberry Finn* by Mark Twain, Huck and Jim continue living together on Jackson’s Island. They discuss the things they found in the floating house, including some useful supplies. Jim warns Huck that touching a snakeskin brings bad luck. However, Huck does not take the warning seriously and later kills a rattlesnake. He places the dead snake near Jim’s blanket, and another snake bites Jim when it comes looking for its mate.

Jim suffers from the snakebite, but he eventually recovers. Huck feels guilty because his actions caused Jim to get hurt. Meanwhile, Huck becomes curious about what is happening in the nearby town. He disguises himself as a girl and goes to town to gather information. During his visit, he learns that people suspect Jim of murdering Huck and are searching for him on Jackson’s Island.

This chapter highlights Huck’s curiosity, Jim’s superstitions, and the dangers they face while trying to remain hidden.

Part 2 : The chapter clue hunt
----------------------------------
(Comprehension and critical Thinking)
Write answers of following on Literature Notebook (NB - 1)
1. Why do Huck and Jim explore the floating house? What do they discover inside?
Huck and Jim explore the floating house because they want to find useful things they can take for their survival. Inside, they discover various items, including furniture, clothes, and other supplies. They also find the body of a dead man, so Jim prevents Huck from looking at his face.

2. Why does Jim believe that touching a snakeskin brings bad luck? What happens after Huck plays a trick on him?
Jim believes that touching a snakeskin brings bad luck because of his superstitions. Huck does not take his warning seriously and plays a trick on Jim by placing a dead rattlesnake near his bed. Later, the snake’s mate comes looking for it and bites Jim. Jim suffers from the snakebite, and Huck feels guilty for his foolish trick.

3. Which event or character's decision in these chapters surprised you the most? Explain your answer using details from the story.
The event that surprised me the most was Huck’s decision to disguise himself as a girl to gather information in the town. He wanted to find out whether people were searching for him and Jim. By disguising himself, Huck was able to learn that people suspected Jim of murdering him and planned to search Jackson’s Island. I was surprised by Huck’s cleverness and courage because he took a risk to get important information. This event shows that Huck is resourceful and can think quickly in difficult situations.

Part 3 : Creative Project - THE LOST EXPLORERS'S FIELD JOURNAL
(to be displayed on the classroom bulletin board)
One attractive explorer's field journal on 8, 9, 10
Material required:
One A4-sized,markers or crayons.
colour pencil, eraser and rule.
other decorative (optional)

Must Include All Five elements:
1. The explorer's Map
Draw a map of Jackson's Island and the nearby river based on the story. Label at least four relevant features or locations.
2. Word Treasures
Choose three difficult words from the assigned chapters. Write their meaning and add a small sketch or symbol to represent each word.
3. A Moment Frozen in Time
Draw one memorable scene from chapters 8,9 or 10. Add a caption of 2 - 3 sentences explaining what is happening and why the moment is important.
4. A secret Diary Entry
Imagine you are Huck. Write a diary entry of approximately 60-80 words describing one event from the assigned chapters. Include your thoughts and feeling about what happended.
5. The next chapter prediction
Predict what you think might happen next in the story. Write your prediction and include at last one clue from the assigned chapters to support your idea.

Presentation Guidelines:
Give your field journal an interesting title.
use neat handwriting and organize the sections clearly
include relevant illustrations and a decorative border.
mare sure all written work is readable.
write your full name and class on the back of the sheet.
Ensure that all information is based on the assigned chapters.
